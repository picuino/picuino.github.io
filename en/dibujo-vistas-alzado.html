1. Front view¶

The front view is the view we choose as the main view for representing a part. It is the representation of the part as seen from the front.

The front view mainly shows the height and width of the part, but it does not show its depth.

In the three-dimensional view, the front view is usually indicated by an arrow showing the direction from which the part is viewed.

We choose as the front view the view that best represents the shape of the part, showing as many details as possible and avoiding hidden lines whenever possible.

The edges that form the front view are also shown. These are the lines that we need to draw in the view.

In these exercises, surfaces are not drawn or filled with color: only visible edges are represented using thick continuous lines.
